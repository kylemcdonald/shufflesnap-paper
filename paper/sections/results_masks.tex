Table~\ref{tab:masks} and Figure~\ref{fig:masks} cover non-square targets.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Convex masks.} On disks and rings, the default preset stays within 0.07\% of
  the optimum, fast within 0.14\% and quality within 0.025\%. BSP-OT is 2.5--9.3\% above it
  and RasterFairy 11--54\%.
\item \textbf{The S-shaped mask} (the blocky, rectilinear S of the earlier ShuffleSnap
  work: three horizontal bars joined by vertical bars on alternating sides) is where the
  schedule matters most. Coarse strides can route mass across its bays
  (Figure~\ref{fig:failures}c). The default reaches 1.4\% with uniform points and 0.26\%
  with the spiral, against 9.3\% and 4.8\% for BSP-OT with 64 plans, and the quality preset
  0.88\% and 0.19\%. The fast preset is at 3.3\% with the spiral, against 12.5\% for BSP-OT
  with 16 plans, but with uniform points it is the one mask case where BSP-OT wins at equal
  time: 17\% against 12\%.
\item \textbf{Surplus cells.} When the solver must also choose the empty cells, uniform points
  with less than one row of surplus are the hardest case: 3.1\% for the default, 6.3\% for
  fast and 1.4\% for quality. With 10\% surplus the default stays within 0.91\% on every
  input. Surplus is where the comparators suffer most. With 10\% surplus, BSP-OT's partial
  matching is 18--109\% above the optimum, and RasterFairy, which picks the empty cells at
  random, is 72--350\% above it. With less than one row of surplus, they are at 5--90\% and
  17--57\%.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Prime counts.} A prime number of points never fills a rectangle.
Table~\ref{tab:primes} compares the two ways of handling this. With an exact-fit grid,
the results match the square case: 0.85--1.2\% for the default on uniform points, and
0.002--0.003\% on the mixture. Leaving the solver to pick fewer than 200 empty cells in a
full rectangle is much harder on uniform points: the default reaches 5.1--5.7\% there, against
0.85--1.2\% with the exact fit; fast reaches 10.3--10.9\% and quality 3.0--3.6\%. BSP-OT's
partial matching is worse still, at 147--152\%. For layouts where the empty cells' position does
not matter, an exact-fit mask is therefore both simpler and better handled by every method.
