\begin{abstract}
Snapping each point of a 2D embedding (such as a UMAP layout) to its own cell of a grid,
while minimizing total squared displacement, is a linear assignment problem. Dense solvers
cannot handle it beyond a few tens of thousands of points. We develop and test
\ShuffleSnap, a local search inspired by Shellsort. At a decreasing sequence of strides, it
splits the grid into interleaved subgrids and solves small exact assignment problems in
shifted $6\times6$ windows of each.

To measure true optimality gaps, we built a certified exact solver. It combines min-cost
flow on sparse candidate edges with a Lagrangian lower bound priced over all cells. Started
from \ShuffleSnap, it proved optimality on every instance we tried, up to $10^6$ points.

On held-out instances with $10^5$ points, the baseline as specified is within 0.2\% of the
optimum on nine of eleven distributions. It takes about 5\,s on one core and 0.35\,s on 24
threads. A finer stride sequence (ratio $\sqrt2$) with $8\times8$ windows reduces the excess
a further 2.6--14$\times$ on ten of the eleven. BSP-OT is faster but typically 10--350$\times$ further from
optimal. RasterFairy is 17--425\% above optimal on most inputs. A dense exact solver
already needs up to 6 minutes at $10^4$ points.

The method's limits are structural. Its error consists of long closed loops of one-cell
shifts that no window can remove, and we construct inputs on which its relative gap is
unbounded. Uniform points (2.5\% at $10^5$, 5.3\% at $10^6$), jittered lattices (33\%), a
concave mask (18\%, worse than BSP-OT) and surplus cells remain hard. Structured starts
make results worse, and polishing at coarser strides barely changes the result. Warm-started from
\ShuffleSnap, the certified solver reaches proven optima in seconds to minutes at $10^5$
points, which narrows the practical case for the heuristic.

On real UMAP embeddings, displacement and neighbourhood preservation diverge. BSP-OT is
within 2--7\% of the optimal displacement but halves the fraction of preserved grid
neighbours. We release both the implementation and all experiment code and raw results.
\end{abstract}
