\subsection{Quality at a matched time budget}
\label{sec:anytime}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_anytime_t1.pdf}
  \caption{Excess over the certified optimum against elapsed time, one thread. \ShuffleSnap's
  step curves record the exact cost after every phase, so each shows the result that a time
  budget ending at that moment would return. BSP-OT is run separately with 1, 2, 4, \dots,
  1024 plans (labels at 1, 16 and 1024). RasterFairy upstream is shown with exact leaf
  blocks of 1, 50, 200 and 1000 points. Instance seed 1. The same plot with 24 threads is
  in the appendix (Figure~\ref{fig:anytime24}).}
  \label{fig:anytime}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:anytime} compares the methods at matched time budgets. BSP-OT improves
slowly with more plans and levels off far from the optimum. From 1 to 16 to 1024 plans,
its excess drops from 249\% to 53\% to 28\% on uniform points, from 28\% to 3.1\% to
2.1\% on the mixture, and from 16\% to 3.6\% to 1.6\% on MNIST. The 1024-plan runs take
31--46\,s on one thread. The fast \ShuffleSnap\ preset passes that best BSP-OT result after
0.2--0.5\,s and the default after 0.5--1.3\,s. They finish at 3.7\%, 0.002\% and 0.027\%
(fast) and at 0.97\%, 0.0005\% and 0.002\% (default). BSP-OT with 1--8 plans is the better
choice only for budgets below 0.12--0.36\,s on one thread, when \ShuffleSnap\ has finished
only its coarsest levels. With 24 threads, the fast preset is ahead at every budget for which
BSP-OT returns a result. Larger windows are slower not only per round but also to any cost a smaller window
reaches: the quality preset needed 2.8--7.4\,s to reach the cost at which the fast preset
stops after 0.7--0.9\,s, and the default 1.4--2.6\,s. A larger window pays off only at
costs that a smaller one never reaches. RasterFairy's leaf size hardly matters except on uniform points
(73\% with no exact leaves, 45\% with 1000-point leaves).
