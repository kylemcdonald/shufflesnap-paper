\subsection{Ablations on held-out instances}
\label{sec:ablation}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_ablation.pdf}
  \caption{Held-out ablations. Each panel shows mean excess over the certified optimum against
  median time (24 threads, three instance seeds). The circle marks the default ($8\times8$
  windows, stride ratio $\sqrt2$). Lines vary an ordered setting: the window side with
  $\sqrt2$ strides (dark) or halving strides (light), labelled at 4 and 24; and the stride
  ratio at $w=8$ (diamonds; r3 and r1.1 mark the extremes). Markers show categorical changes
  to the default: bisection or row-sort starts, and 2 or 9 shifted tilings per round instead
  of 4. A single tiling is off the chart on uniform points (104\%). Solver choice and skipping
  unchanged windows do not change quality; they are listed with all other numbers in
  Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.}
  \label{fig:ablation}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:ablation} varies one design choice at a time, starting from the default,
on instances that were not used for any design decision.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Window side} is the main quality knob. With $\sqrt2$ strides, uniform excess
  falls from 3.8\% ($4\times4$) to 0.84\% ($8\times8$), 0.44\% ($12\times12$), 0.19\%
  ($16\times16$) and 0.09\% ($24\times24$). Time rises from 0.06\,s to 1.6\,s on uniform points
  and from 0.06\,s to 9.3\,s on the mixture. With halving strides the whole curve lies higher
  (e.g.\ 1.5\% at $8\times8$).
\item \textbf{Stride ratio}. $\sqrt2$ is the best ratio on uniform points and MNIST. On the
  mixture, 1.68 and 1.19 are better (0.0017\% and 0.0020\% against 0.0023\%). Ratios of 2 and 3
  are worse on all three inputs (uniform: 1.5\% and 2.4\% against 0.84\%).
\item \textbf{Coarse levels are essential}. Capping the first stride at 16, 8, 4, 2 or 1 (the
  last means stride-1 windows only, from the random start) made results up to 90 times
  worse and up to 45 times slower. On uniform points the default reaches 0.84\% in 0.12\,s;
  capped at 8 it reaches 9.6\% in 0.21\,s, and with stride 1 only 21\% in 0.47\,s. Without
  long-range moves, stride-1 windows need many rounds to repair a random start, and they
  stall far from the optimum.
\item \textbf{Start}. A random start beats bisection on all three inputs (uniform: 0.84\%
  against 22.9\%) and row sorting on uniform points and MNIST (12.4\% and 0.0031\% against
  0.84\% and 0.0020\%). Bisection's straight seams are what windows cannot undo. On the
  mixture, a row-sorted start was better (0.0011\% against 0.0023\%).
\item \textbf{Shifted tilings are essential}. With a single fixed tiling, points can never
  cross a window border (uniform: 104\%). Two diagonal shifts give 2.0\%, the default four
  0.84\%, and nine 0.70\% at twice the time. A second round per level gives 0.70\% at 1.4
  times the time, less than a larger window gives for the same time ($10\times10$: 0.54\% at
  1.5 times).
\item \textbf{Window solver}. All five solvers are exact, so the results are identical
  up to ties. Relative to the Hungarian method with a greedy start, the geometric solver
  (Section~\ref{sec:geosolver}) is 2.0--2.4$\times$ faster on uniform points and
  5.0--5.4$\times$ faster on the mixture and MNIST. The auction with an exact finish is
  2.1--3.4$\times$ faster on the clustered inputs but 1.04--1.3$\times$ slower on uniform
  points. The plain Hungarian method is up to 1.2$\times$ slower, and Jonker--Volgenant
  3--12$\times$ slower.
\item \textbf{Skipping unchanged windows} leaves results unchanged and saves 22--24\% of the time.
\item \textbf{Post-processing does not pay.} Three extra cycles of stride-2 and stride-1
  rounds after convergence changed nothing on seven of the nine instances and removed at
  most 5\% of the (already tiny) excess on the other two, both MNIST. Merging four
  independent default runs, keeping the cheaper of the two assignments on every connected
  component of their union (the rule BSP-OT uses to merge plans), lowered the uniform excess
  from 0.84\% to 0.56\% at 4.1 times the time. The quality preset does better (0.44\%) at 2.1
  times the time.
\end{itemize}
